# UniVerse — Project Brief

## 1. Project Overview

**UniVerse** is a mobile-first digital campus platform designed initially for **Bayero University Kano (BUK) students**.

The goal is to create a single digital space where BUK students can **connect, communicate, discover opportunities, access useful campus information, participate in communities, and navigate student life**.

The product should feel less like a traditional university portal and more like a **modern social/community platform built specifically around campus life**.

### Brand

**UniVerse**
*by BUK Students*

The name represents a digital "universe" surrounding student life — people, academics, communities, events, opportunities, businesses, and everyday campus activities.

---

# 2. The Problem

BUK students currently rely on a fragmented collection of platforms and channels for campus-related activities:

* WhatsApp groups
* Telegram groups
* Faculty/department groups
* Social media
* Word of mouth
* Notice boards
* Separate platforms for buying/selling
* Separate channels for events and opportunities
* Informal networks for finding accommodation, study partners, services, etc.

This makes it difficult for students to discover relevant information and connect with the right people.

UniVerse aims to bring these experiences into **one student-centered ecosystem**.

---

# 3. Target Users

### Primary Users

**BUK Students**

Including:

* Undergraduate students
* Postgraduate students
* Fresh students
* Returning students
* Final-year students

### Potential Future Users

The platform may eventually expand to:

* Student organizations
* Campus businesses
* Clubs and associations
* Event organizers
* University-related services
* Other Nigerian universities

However, the initial product should remain **strongly focused on BUK**.

---

# 4. Core Product Vision

UniVerse should become:

> **The digital campus for BUK students.**

A student should be able to open UniVerse and quickly answer questions such as:

* What's happening on campus?
* What's happening in my department?
* What events are coming up?
* Where can I find study partners?
* Who can help me with this course?
* Is anyone selling what I need?
* Where can I find a particular service?
* What opportunities are available?
* What's trending among students?
* How can I connect with other students?
* Where can I find important campus information?

The application should prioritize **discovery, community, usefulness, and connection**.

---

# 5. Core Features

The exact feature set can evolve, but the initial product should be structured around the following areas.

## A. Student Profiles

Each student should have a profile containing relevant information such as:

* Name
* Profile photo
* Faculty
* Department
* Level
* Interests
* Skills
* Short bio

Students should be able to discover other students based on shared interests, faculties, departments, courses, communities, etc.

Privacy should be considered from the beginning. Students should control what information is publicly visible.

---

## B. Campus Feed

A central feed where students can discover relevant campus content.

Possible content types:

* Student posts
* Announcements
* Events
* Opportunities
* Questions
* Discussions
* Campus news
* Trending topics

The feed should feel familiar to modern social applications while remaining focused on **useful campus information rather than generic social media content**.

---

## C. Communities

Students should be able to join communities based on:

* Faculty
* Department
* Level
* Course
* Student organizations
* Clubs
* Interests
* Hobbies
* Academic groups

Examples:

* Computer Science 300 Level
* BUK Developers
* Medical Students Community
* Entrepreneurship Community
* Football Community
* Photography Community

Communities should provide a structured alternative to having hundreds of disconnected WhatsApp groups.

---

## D. Events

A dedicated area for discovering campus events.

Events could include:

* Student events
* Club events
* Seminars
* Workshops
* Competitions
* Religious/community events
* Career events
* Social activities

Students should be able to:

* Discover events
* View event details
* Save events
* Register where applicable
* Share events
* Receive reminders

---

## E. Opportunities

A dedicated discovery section for opportunities such as:

* Scholarships
* Internships
* Fellowships
* Competitions
* Hackathons
* Jobs
* Campus opportunities
* Training programs

The goal is to prevent useful opportunities from getting buried inside WhatsApp groups.

---

## F. Student Marketplace

A campus-focused marketplace where students can buy and sell items/services.

Potential categories:

* Electronics
* Books
* Fashion
* Food
* Accommodation-related items
* School materials
* Services
* Freelance/student services

The marketplace should initially prioritize **student-to-student transactions**.

Safety, reporting, moderation, and scam prevention should be considered as core requirements.

---

## G. Lost & Found

A dedicated system for reporting:

* Lost items
* Found items

Students should be able to post details, images, location, and relevant information.

This can become one of the platform's highly practical campus-specific features.

---

## H. Study & Academic Connections

Students should be able to find others for:

* Study groups
* Course discussions
* Academic collaboration
* Project partners
* Study partners

The system could eventually support course-specific communities and matching.

---

## I. Campus Services & Directory

A searchable directory of useful campus-related services.

Potential categories:

* Food vendors
* Printing/photocopy services
* Tailors
* Barbers
* Phone/electronics services
* Transportation
* Accommodation services
* Student businesses
* Other campus services

This section can eventually become a major ecosystem within UniVerse.

---

## J. Messaging & Notifications

Students should eventually be able to communicate through:

* Direct messaging
* Community/group discussions
* Event notifications
* Important announcements
* Opportunity notifications
* Marketplace notifications

Notifications should be useful and configurable rather than becoming spam.

---

# 6. Personalization

UniVerse should not treat every student the same.

During onboarding, collect enough information to personalize the experience:

* Faculty
* Department
* Level
* Interests
* Courses
* Communities

The home experience should then prioritize content relevant to the individual student.

For example, a Computer Science 300-level student should naturally see more relevant CS communities, opportunities, events, and discussions.

---

# 7. User Experience Direction

The application should feel:

* Modern
* Clean
* Fast
* Youthful
* Community-driven
* Intuitive
* Professional
* Nigerian/African without relying on clichés

Avoid making the interface look like a traditional university management system.

The design inspiration should come more from **modern consumer/social applications** than from existing university portals.

The experience should be mobile-first and optimized for users who may have:

* Limited data
* Mid-range Android devices
* Unstable internet connections
* Slower network speeds

Performance is therefore a product requirement, not just a technical consideration.

---

# 8. Navigation Concept

A possible primary navigation structure:

**Home**
Personalized campus feed.

**Discover**
Communities, events, opportunities, marketplace, services, etc.

**Communities**
Student groups and discussions.

**Create**
Create a post, event, listing, opportunity, etc.

**Profile**
Student profile, saved content, settings, activity.

The exact navigation can be changed if UX research suggests a better structure.

---

# 9. Trust, Safety & Moderation

Because UniVerse is a student community, trust is extremely important.

The platform should consider:

* Student verification
* Reporting
* Blocking
* Content moderation
* Community moderation
* Marketplace reporting
* Scam prevention
* Spam prevention
* Admin moderation tools

Where possible, users should be verified as genuine BUK students.

The system should also distinguish between:

**Verified student**

**Verified organization**

**Verified business/service**

**Platform administrator**

---

# 10. Admin Platform

UniVerse should have an administrative system for managing the ecosystem.

Administrators should eventually be able to:

* Manage users
* Verify students
* Manage communities
* Moderate posts
* Moderate marketplace listings
* Manage events
* Manage opportunities
* Handle reports
* Suspend users
* Manage categories
* View platform analytics
* Manage announcements

The admin experience should be separate from the student mobile experience.

---

# 11. MVP Philosophy

Do not attempt to build every feature at once.

The first version should prove the central hypothesis:

> **Will BUK students consistently use a single platform to discover and participate in campus life?**

The MVP should therefore focus on the strongest loop:

**Join → Build Profile → Discover → Connect → Participate → Return**

A sensible MVP could prioritize:

1. Authentication/student verification
2. Student profiles
3. Personalized home feed
4. Posts/discussions
5. Communities
6. Events
7. Opportunities
8. Notifications
9. Basic search/discovery
10. Reporting/moderation
11. Basic admin dashboard

Marketplace, advanced messaging, services directory, sophisticated matching, and other features can be introduced progressively.

---

# 12. Future Vision

UniVerse should be designed so that BUK is the **starting point, not necessarily the endpoint**.

If successful, the platform could eventually support other universities.

For example:

**UniVerse**

* BUK
* ABU
* KUST
* UNN
* UI
* FUTMINNA
* etc.

Each university could have its own campus ecosystem while sharing the underlying UniVerse platform.

This means the architecture should avoid unnecessarily hard-coding the product around BUK.

However, **do not sacrifice the BUK experience in the MVP just to prepare for hypothetical expansion**.

---

# 13. Product Personality

UniVerse should feel like:

> **A digital home for student life.**

Not:

> Another university portal.

Not:

> Another generic social media application.

Not:

> Another WhatsApp replacement.

Its differentiation should come from being **deeply contextualized around campus life**.

Every major feature should answer one question:

> **Does this make life better, easier, more connected, or more interesting for a student?**

---

# 14. Initial Success Metrics

The product should eventually measure:

* Number of verified students
* Daily active users
* Weekly active users
* Student retention
* Number of communities created
* Community participation
* Posts created
* Events discovered/registered
* Opportunities viewed
* Marketplace activity
* Messages/connections
* Reports/moderation incidents
* Percentage of users returning after onboarding

The most important early metric should be **student retention and meaningful engagement**, not simply downloads.

---

# 15. Design Principle

The most important principle for the entire project:

> **Build for students first, not for features.**

Every screen should be simple, useful, and purposeful.

UniVerse should eventually feel like something BUK students naturally open every day because it contains information, people, opportunities, and communities that are relevant to their lives.
